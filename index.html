Sum 1: Quadratic Trinomial (x2 + 9x + 18)
Factorise: x² + 9x + 18
Step 1: Find two numbers whose product is 18 and sum is 9.
(Factors of 18: 6 and 3 → 6 × 3 = 18 and 6 + 3 = 9)
Step 2: Split the middle term: x2 + 6x + 3x + 18
Step 3: Factor by grouping: x(x + 6) + 3(x + 6)
Final Answer: (x + 6)(x + 3) ✨
Sum 2: Quadratic Trinomial with Coefficient (3x2 - 7x - 6)
Factorise: 3x² - 7x - 6
Step 1: Multiply coefficient of x2 and constant term: 3 x (-6) = -18. Find two numbers whose product is -18 and sum is -7.
(Numbers are -9 and 2 → -9 x 2 = -18 and -9 + 2 = -7)
Step 2: Split middle term: 3x2 - 9x + 2x - 6
Step 3: Factor by grouping: 3x(x - 3) + 2(x - 3)
Final Answer: (x - 3)(3x + 2) ✨
Sum 3: Difference of Cubes Identity (x3 - 27)
Factorise: x³ - 27
Step 1: Express as difference of cubes: x3 - 33
Step 2: Apply identity a3 - b3 = (a - b)(a² + ab + b²) where a=x and b=3.
Step 3: Expand: (x - 3)(x² + (x)(3) + 3²)
Final Answer: (x - 3)(x² + 3x + 9) ✨
Sum 4: Standard Quadratic (x² - 10x + 21)
Factorise: x² - 10x + 21
Step 1: Find two numbers whose product is 21 and sum is -10.
(Numbers are -7 and -3 → -7 x -3 = 21 and -7 + (-3) = -10)
Step 2: Split middle term: x² - 7x - 3x + 21
Step 3: Factor by grouping: x(x - 7) - 3(x - 7)
Final Answer: (x - 7)(x - 3) ✨

1. What is a Term? (The Train Carriage)

A term is like a single carriage in our algebraic train. It has a number (coefficient) and a variable with an exponent (like 3x2). The plus or minus signs act as couplers linking them together into a full train!

Example Train: In 5x2 - 4x + 7, there are 3 terms: 5x2, -4x, and 7, linked together by signs.

2. What exactly is a Polynomial?

A polynomial is a mathematical expression made up of variables and constants combined using addition, subtraction, multiplication, and non-negative integer exponents.

Fun Fact: A standalone number like 7 is a single-carriage constant train (7 = 7x0) because x0 = 1. Even zero is a polynomial!

3. So what is NOT a Polynomial?

Don't get tricked! A train crashes (not a polynomial) if it has:

  • Variables in the denominator (e.g., 1/x or x-1).
  • Fractional or negative exponents (e.g., √x = x1/2).
  • Variables inside radical signs (e.g., √x+2).

The number you divide by is the Divisor! If the division leaves a Remainder of 0, then that Divisor is a Factor! (The quotient is its factor partner).

⚖️ The Zero Balance Rule

When solving equations with algebra tiles, the golden rule is Zero Balance: a positive tile (+1) and a negative tile (-1) cancel each other out completely to equal zero (+1 + (-1) = 0).

📈 Number Line & Tile Balancing Act

Why do we take a positive tile if we are introducing a negative tile (and vice versa)? On a number line, if you are at position $+3$ and want to reach 0, you must add -3 steps in the opposite direction. To keep an equation balanced on both sides, whatever tile action you take on one side (e.g., adding a negative tile), you must introduce its opposite positive counterpart to preserve the zero balance!
